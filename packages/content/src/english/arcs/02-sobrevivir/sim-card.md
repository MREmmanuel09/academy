## Scene 2: Asking for help (4 min)

> **Maria:** "Hi! I need a SIM card, please. I have an unlocked phone."

> **Shop Assistant:** "Sure thing. Are you looking for a pay-as-you-go plan, or a monthly contract?"

> **Maria:** "What's the difference?"

> **Shop Assistant:** "Pay-as-you-go means you top up your credit when you run out. A monthly contract means you pay the same amount every month and get a set amount of data and minutes."

> **Maria:** "I think pay-as-you-go is better for me. I don't use my phone that much."

> **Shop Assistant:** "OK, that's £10 for the SIM, and you can top up with £5, £10, or £20. I'd recommend £20 to start. You'll get 100 minutes, 500 texts, and 2GB of data."

> **Maria:** "That sounds good. Can I have the £20 top-up, please?"

> **Shop Assistant:** "Sure. Do you have a UK ID? We need to register the SIM."

> **Maria:** "Yes, I have my passport."

> **Shop Assistant:** "Perfect. Just fill in this form with your name, address, and date of birth. And we need a UK address."

> **Maria:** "I have a hotel address. Will that work?"

> **Shop Assistant:** "For now, yes. But when you get a permanent address, you'll need to update it."

### Grammar note: comparatives

> "Pay-as-you-go is **better for me**."

We use comparatives to compare two things:
- For short adjectives: add -er (cheap → cheaper, fast → faster)
- For long adjectives: use "more" (more expensive, more reliable)
- For irregular: good → better, bad → worse, far → farther

| Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| cheap | cheaper |
| fast | faster |
| good | better |
| bad | worse |
| expensive | more expensive |
| reliable | more reliable |

To compare with "than": "X is cheaper than Y."

### Grammar note: "since" and "for" with present perfect

> "I'll set up international calling for you **since** you're from Spain."

We use **present perfect** with "since" (a point in time) and "for" (a duration):
- "I have lived here **for** 3 months." (duration)
- "I have lived here **since** May." (point in time)
- "I have been a teacher **since** 2018." (point in time)

This emphasizes the continuity or the relevance of the past to the present.

## Cultural note: mobile phones in the UK

> In the UK, it's very common to use **pay-as-you-go** plans, especially for short stays or people who don't use their phone much. The major networks are EE, O2, Vodafone, and Three. SIM-only deals are popular because they're cheaper than contracts with a phone included.

> **"Top up"** in British English = "recargar" in Spanish. You can top up at any shop, online, or by phone.
