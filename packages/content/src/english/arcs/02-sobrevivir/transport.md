### Grammar note: imperative for instructions

> "Stand on the right side of the escalator. People walk on the left."

We use **imperatives** (base form, no subject) for instructions:
- "Stand on the right." (you stand on the right)
- "Open the door." (you open the door)
- "Mind the gap." (you be careful of the gap)

This is the simplest and most direct way to give instructions in English.

In a Tube announcement, "Mind the gap" means "Be careful of the gap."

### Grammar note: past simple for completed actions

> "I just **took** the Tube for the first time."
> "I just **tapped** my card."

We use past simple for completed actions in the past:
- "I **arrived** at 5 PM."
- "She **tapped** her card."
- "We **had** dinner at 7."

Regular verbs: add -ed (tap → tapped, arrive → arrived)
Irregular verbs (must memorize): take → took, make → made, have → had, go → went, see → saw, do → did, come → came, give → gave

## Cultural note: London Underground etiquette

> **"Stand on the right, walk on the left"** is the rule on Tube escalators. Breaking this rule is one of the most common ways to upset Londoners.

> **"Mind the gap"** has been a recorded announcement on the Tube since 1968. It is one of the most iconic phrases in London.

> **No eye contact, no talking** is the unwritten rule on the Tube. Londoners often read books or look at their phones. Talking to strangers is unusual unless you need help.

> **Always tap in AND tap out** on the same journey, otherwise you'll be charged the maximum fare.
