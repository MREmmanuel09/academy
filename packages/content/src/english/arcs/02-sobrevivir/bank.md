## Scene 2: Explaining what she needs (3 min)

> **Maria:** "Hi! I'd like to open a current account, please. I just moved to the UK and I need an account for my salary."

> **Bank Advisor:** "Welcome to the UK! I'd be happy to help. Do you have a UK address and proof of ID?"

> **Maria:** "Yes, I have my passport. For the address, I have my hotel address, but I'm looking for a flat. Is that a problem?"

> **Bank Advisor:** "For opening the account, no. But we'll need a permanent address within 3 months. Where are you staying now?"

> **Maria:** "At The Strand Hotel."

> **Bank Advisor:** "OK, we can use that for now. We can update it later when you have a permanent address."

### Grammar note: "would like" for polite requests

> "I**'d** like to open a current account, please."

"I'd like" = "I would like" — a polite way to make a request or express a wish.

| Polite | Casual |
|---|---|
| "I would like a coffee, please." | "I want a coffee." |
| "Would you help me?" | "Can you help me?" |
| "I'd like to know more." | "I want to know more." |

In a bank or formal setting, always use "would like" — it sounds much more polite.

## Scene 3: Choosing the account type (4 min)

> **Bank Advisor:** "We have two main account types. The **Classic Account** is free, but you need to pay in at least £500 per month. The **Select Account** has a £10 monthly fee, but it includes travel insurance and a higher overdraft limit."

> **Maria:** "What's an overdraft?"

> **Bank Advisor:** "An overdraft is when you spend more money than you have in your account. The bank covers the difference, but you pay interest on it. The Classic Account has a £100 overdraft. The Select Account has a £500 overdraft."

> **Maria:** "I think the Classic Account is better for me. I don't travel much for work yet, and I don't need a big overdraft."

> **Bank Advisor:** "Good choice. Let me set that up for you. I'll need your passport, your UK address, and we need to do a quick credit check. Is that OK?"

> **Maria:** "Yes, that's fine."

### Grammar note: comparatives with "than"

> "The Classic Account has a £100 overdraft. The Select Account has a £500 overdraft."

To compare two things, we use:
- "X is [comparative] **than** Y."
- "X has a [comparative] [noun] **than** Y."

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| "X is cheaper than Y." | X costs less than Y. |
| "X has a higher overdraft than Y." | X has more overdraft than Y. |
| "X is more reliable than Y." | X is more reliable than Y. |

## Scene 4: Setting up the account (3 min)

> **Bank Advisor:** "Perfect. Your account is set up. Here's your debit card and your PIN is being sent by post to your address. It should arrive in 3-5 working days."

> **Maria:** "Great. Can I use the card right away?"

> **Bank Advisor:** "Yes, but you can't withdraw cash until you have the PIN. You can use contactless payment and online banking immediately."

> **Maria:** "Perfect. How do I set up the online banking?"

> **Bank Advisor:** "Download our app and register with your account number and sort code. The sort code is 20-71-64 and your account number is 12345678."

> **Maria:** "Sort code 20-71-64, account 12345678. Got it."

> **Bank Advisor:** "You'll receive a one-time password by text message to your mobile to confirm. Is 07700 900 482 the right number?"

> **Maria:** "Yes, perfect."

> **Bank Advisor:** "Excellent. Your account is fully set up. Welcome to the bank!"

### Grammar note: present simple for instructions and routines

> "**Download** our app and **register** with your account number."
> "You **receive** a one-time password by text."

We use present simple (base form) for:
- Instructions: "Open the door."
- Routines: "I check my email every morning."
- Permanent facts: "Water boils at 100 degrees."

In step-by-step instructions, the present simple is used as a kind of universal "you" — even when speaking to one person.

## Cultural note: banking in the UK

> UK bank accounts have two key numbers: the **sort code** (6 digits, format: XX-XX-XX) and the **account number** (8 digits). You need both to receive money from abroad. They appear on every cheque and on your online banking.

> **Contactless payment** is the norm. You can pay for almost anything under £100 by tapping your card or phone. No PIN required for small amounts.

> Most UK banks have a **"buffer" or small overdraft** included with current accounts. It's not a "feature" you have to apply for separately.
