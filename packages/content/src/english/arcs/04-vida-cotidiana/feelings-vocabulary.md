# Episode 4.5: Naming what you feel

**Duration:** ~12 minutes
**Target level:** B1
**Setting:** Maria has been in London 4 months. She's feeling a complicated mix of things. A conversation with Sam at the pub.

---

## Characters

- **Maria** — Reflecting on her first months.
- **Sam** — 8 years in London. Has been where she is.

---

## Scene 1: Maria opens up (4 min)

> **Maria:** "Sam, can I be honest with you for a sec?"

> **Sam:** "Always."

> **Maria:** "I'm... I don't know. Some days I feel great. Like, I love my job, I've made friends, I'm learning loads. But other days I just feel kind of... flat? Not sad exactly. Just empty."

> **Sam:** "Yeah. I remember that. Took me about six months to feel settled. Four months in, I was up and down."

> **Maria:** "Is that normal?"

> **Sam:** "Totally normal. It's a lot, moving countries. Even when it's a good move."

> **Maria:** "I feel guilty for feeling bad. Like, I chose this. I should be grateful."

> **Sam:** "That's the worst thing you can do to yourself. You can be grateful AND homesick. Both at the same time."

### Cultura: la cultura de la salud mental en UK

> **Narrator:** "The UK is more open about mental health than Spain or Latin America. People use words like 'flat', 'anxious', 'burnt out' openly. Companies often have mental health days. The NHS runs campaigns like 'It's OK to say'. This doesn't mean everyone is constantly opening up — but the door is more open than in many other countries. Talking to Sam about how she feels is normal here. Doing the same in Spain might feel dramatic."

> **Sam's voiceover:** "When I moved back from Australia, I was crying in a supermarket for no reason. Took me a year. You're not broken, Maria. You're just adjusting."

## Scene 2: Naming more feelings (4 min)

> **Maria:** "What else might I feel? I want to be ready."

> **Sam:** "Honestly, expect a bit of a rollercoaster. There's a word we use for it — 'culture shock' — but it's not just about being shocked. It's the whole adjustment."

> **Maria:** "Like what?"

> **Sam:** "Like, the first month: everything is exciting. New! Different! You love it."

> **Maria:** "Check."

> **Sam:** "Second month: you start noticing the things that bother you. The food's not the same. You miss your family. The way people do things here feels weird."

> **Maria:** "Check."

> **Sam:** "Third and fourth months: the dip. You're past the honeymoon, you're past the frustration, and now you're just... adjusting. It's quiet work. No big highs, no big lows, just slow building."

> **Maria:** "That's where I am."

> **Sam:** "Then around month six or seven, things start to click. The supermarket doesn't feel foreign anymore. You stop translating in your head. You start feeling like you live here, not just visiting."

### Frase clave

**"It's a rollercoaster"** = es una montaña rusa (emocional). Se usa para advertir que vendrán altibajos.

**"Things start to click"** = las cosas empiezan a tener sentido. "Click" es ese momento de "ah, ya entiendo cómo funciona esto".

## Scene 3: What to do about it (4 min)

> **Maria:** "What helped you? In the dip?"

> **Sam:** "Honestly? Three things. One: I found a routine. Gym Tuesday, pub Tuesday, groceries Sunday. Just small things that were mine."

> **Maria:** "Routine. OK."

> **Sam:** "Two: I made one friend I could be real with. Not loads of people. Just one person I could call when it felt shit."

> **Maria:** "Have you got that now?"

> **Sam:** "Yeah, you're it, mate. Sorry."

> **Maria:** "[laughs] I don't even know you that well!"

> **Sam:** "Exactly. You don't have to know someone for years. Just someone who's honest with you."

> **Maria:** "What was the third thing?"

> **Sam:** "Stop comparing. I kept comparing London to my life before. That made everything worse. Once I let London just be London, not a worse version of home, it got better."

| English | Pronunciation | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| routine | /ruːˈtiːn/ | rutina |
| groceries | /ˈɡrəʊsəriz/ | compra, mandado |

| to call | /kɔːl/ | llamar |
| real | /rɪəl/ | real, auténtico |
| to compare | /kəmˈpeə/ | comparar |
| worse | /wɜːs/ | peor |
| a worse version | /wɜːs ˈvɜːʃən/ | una versión peor |
| just be | /dʒʌst biː/ | simplemente ser |

### Frase clave

**"Just be London"** = permitir que las cosas sean como son, sin compararlas con lo conocido. Es el truco para cualquier adaptación.

**"You're it"** = informal UK. "Tú eres la persona" (en este contexto: "tú eres ese amigo con el que puedo ser real").

### Cultura: hacer amigos cuando estás lejos

> **Narrator:** "Sam just told Maria she's his real friend after four months. In Latin America this would be too soon. In the UK, especially among expats, friendships can deepen faster when you share an experience. You're both far from home. You both know what it's like. That creates a bond that doesn't need years of small talk to develop. This is normal in the expat world. It's not a movie; it's just how life works when you leave home."

## Modismos del episodio

- **"A bit of a rollercoaster"** = una montaña rusa
- **"To be honest"** = siendo honesto
- **"Up and down"** = altibajos
- **"To feel flat"** = sentirse plano, sin energía
- **"To feel shit"** = sentirse mal (fuerte, vulgar, común en UK informal)
- **"Things click"** = las cosas encajan, cobran sentido
- **"To be real with someone"** = ser honesto con alguien
- **"Just be"** = simplemente ser
- **"Mate"** = amigo

## Comprehension quiz (3 min)

1. **"I feel flat" means…**
   - [ ] Happy and energetic
   - [ ] Low energy, deflated, without spark
   - [ ] Physically flat
   - [ ] Hungry

2. **The 4 phases of culture shock are…**
   - [ ] Eat, sleep, work, repeat
   - [ ] Honeymoon, frustration, dip, adjustment
   - [ ] Spring, summer, autumn, winter
   - [ ] Hello, sorry, cheers, bye

3. **"Things start to click" means…**
   - [ ] Things break
   - [ ] Things start to make sense and work
   - [ ] Loud noises
   - [ ] Going home

4. **"To be honest" is used to…**
   - [ ] Lie politely
   - [ ] Soften a sincere or delicate opinion
   - [ ] End calls
   - [ ] Order food

5. **"To feel homesick" means…**
   - [ ] To be sick at home
   - [ ] To miss home while away
   - [ ] To love hospitals
   - [ ] To feel settled

### Answers

1. Low energy, deflated, without spark · 2. Honeymoon, frustration, dip, adjustment · 3. Things start to make sense and work · 4. Soften a sincere or delicate opinion · 5. To miss home while away
