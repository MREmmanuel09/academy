# Episode 4.3: Making a friend

**Duration:** ~14 minutes
**Target level:** A2-B1
**Setting:** Maria is at a local pub quiz night. She meets Sam, a British expat who became a regular.

---

## Characters

- **Maria** — At the pub, alone with her team (Tom and Priya).
- **Sam** — 30s, British, works in tech, has been living in London 8 years.
- **Tom, Priya** — Maria's teammates.

---

## Scene 1: Arriving at the pub (3 min)

**[Audio: pub atmosphere, chatter, music]**

> **Maria:** "This is busy, isn't it?"

> **Tom:** "Always is on Tuesday. Pub quiz night. Half of London is here."

> **Priya:** "Our table's over there. I've already ordered crisps."

> **Maria:** "What kind?"

> **Priya:** "Salt and vinegar. British classic. Hope that's OK?"

> **Maria:** "Sure, I'll try them."

> **Sam:** "Excuse me, is this seat taken?"

> **Tom:** "Go for it. The more the merrier."

## Scene 2: The first conversation (4 min)

> **Sam:** "Hi, I'm Sam. You new here?"

> **Maria:** "Yeah, just moved to London three months ago. Maria."

> **Sam:** "Welcome! How are you finding it?"

> **Maria:** "Good, mostly. The weather is... a lot."

> **Sam:** "Ha. You get used to it. Sort of. Whereabouts are you living?"

> **Maria:** "Shoreditch. You?"

> **Sam:** "Just round the corner, actually. So we're neighbours."

> **Maria:** "Oh, nice. Are you from London originally?"

> **Sam:** "Born and raised. Took me a while to appreciate it, actually. Took a year travelling to make me miss it."

### Cultura: cómo se hacen amigos los adultos

> **Narrator:** "Notice the natural flow. Sam didn't say 'I want to be your friend'. He just asked about her week, where she's from, and where she lives. Adult friendships in big cities are built through repeated small interactions. The pub quiz is a perfect setup — same table, same night, every week. Friendship grows from 'oh, you're here again' to 'do you want to grab a drink after?'."

> **Maria's voiceover:** "It's funny. In Spain, you can become best friends in an afternoon. Here, it feels slower. But maybe that's good? It gives you time to actually like each other."

### Cultura: el pub quiz

> **Narrator:** "Pub quizzes are a UK institution. Most pubs have one weekly. Teams of 4-6, a quizmaster, rounds of 6-10 questions. Categories range from general knowledge to music, geography, film, history. The entry fee is usually £1-2 per person, and the winning team gets the pot or a bar tab. Pub quizzing is how thousands of Britons socialize — it's cheap, it's regular, and the bar provides snacks."

> **Sam:** "Well, we came third. Not bad for a first-timer, Maria."

> **Maria:** "Thanks. I had fun."

> **Sam:** "Same time next week?"

> **Maria:** "Definitely. Are you here most Tuesdays?"

> **Sam:** "Pretty much. It's become a bit of a ritual."

> **Maria:** "Cool. See you next week, then."

> **Sam:** "Cheers. And Maria? Welcome to the neighbourhood."

### Cultura: la amistad adulta en UK

> **Narrator:** "Notice how Sam and Maria didn't exchange phone numbers. They didn't plan a specific outing. They just said 'same time next week'. This is how UK friendships often work: regular, low-key, no pressure. You'll see the same people. You'll start sitting at the same table. Eventually you'll exchange numbers — or you won't, and you'll just see each other every Tuesday. Both are normal."

> **Maria's voiceover:** "I realized I haven't felt lonely since the pub quiz. I have a Tuesday now."

## Frases clave

**"How are you finding it?"** — Cómo lo estás llevando (un trabajo, una ciudad, una situación). Más suave que "Do you like it?"

- "How are you finding the new role?" (cómo te va en el puesto nuevo)
- "How are you finding London life?" (cómo te va en Londres)

**"Same here"** — Yo también (en respuesta a algo que la otra persona acaba de decir).

**"It's become a bit of a ritual"** — Se ha convertido en una especie de ritual. Forma de describir hábitos regulares sin sonar a "rutina aburrida".

**"See you next week, then"** — Nos vemos la próxima semana, entonces. "Then" al final confirma el plan implícito.
