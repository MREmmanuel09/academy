# Episode 6.2: The difficult client

**Duration:** ~14 minutes
**Target level:** B2
**Setting:** A client (Sebastian, German) is being unreasonable about a deadline. Maria has to manage the call.

---

## Characters

- **Maria** — Project manager, hosting the call.
- **Sebastian** — Senior client at a German firm. Aggressive, demanding.
- **Tom** — Maria's boss, on the call for backup.

---

## Scene 1: The setup (2 min)

> **Maria (to herself):** "Right. Big call. Sebastian is upset. Tom is on as backup. Stay calm. Don't take the bait."

> **[Dial tone]**

> **Sebastian:** "Maria. We need to talk about this timeline."

> **Maria:** "Of course, Sebastian. What's on your mind?"

> **Sebastian:** "You said the project would be done by the 15th. Now you're telling me the 22nd. That's a week late. We have a board meeting on the 16th. The board expects the work to be done."

### Vocabulario emergente

| English | Pronunciation | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| to stay calm | /steɪ kɑːm/ | mantener la calma |
| to take the bait | /teɪk ðə beɪt/ | picar el anzuelo, caer en la provocación |
- **"Don't take the bait"** = no piques, no caigas en la provocación
- **"Board meeting"** = reunión de junta directiva
- **"What's on your mind?"** = qué tienes en mente, qué te preocupa

---

## Scene 2: Maria acknowledges (3 min)

> **Maria:** "I hear you, Sebastian. I appreciate you being direct about this. The board meeting is on the 16th, and we originally said we'd deliver on the 15th. That's not what we said."

> **Sebastian:** "Correct. So the question is: can you deliver by the 15th, or not?"

> **Maria:** "I want to be honest with you. I don't think we can. The reason is the data migration — it took longer than we expected. We hit it on Tuesday. If we tried to rush to the 15th, we'd be cutting corners, and the risk of bugs in your system goes up. I don't want to deliver a buggy product to your board."

> **Sebastian:** "So what are you proposing?"

> **Maria:** "I propose we deliver on the 22nd with full quality. The 15th is unrealistic given what we now know. I'd rather be honest with you about that than promise something we can't keep."

### Vocabulario emergente

| English | Pronunciation | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| to acknowledge | /əkˈnɒlɪdʒ/ | reconocer |
| to be direct | /bi dəˈrekt/ | ser directo |
- **"I hear you"** = te escucho (acknowledge)
- **"I appreciate you being direct"** = aprecio tu franqueza
- **"Cutting corners"** = recortar, hacer mal y rápido
- **"Bugs"** = errores (en software)
- **"Buggy product"** = producto lleno de errores
- **"Promise something we can't keep"** = prometer algo que no podemos cumplir

### Cultura: confrontación profesional en UK vs DE

> **Narrator:** "Sebastian is German, and he is direct. In the UK, this can feel aggressive, but Germans often see this as normal business communication. The cultural bridge here is: acknowledge the issue first, then explain your reasoning, then propose a solution. Maria does this well. She doesn't say 'you're wrong' or 'that's not my fault'. She says 'I hear you, here's what we now know, here's what I propose.' The board meeting is real pressure, but rushing creates bigger problems."

---

## Scene 3: Sebastian pushes back (3 min)

> **Sebastian:** "Maria, I appreciate the honesty. But the board is going to ask why we missed the deadline. I need something to give them."

> **Maria:** "That's fair. What if we do a pre-board preview on the 15th? A demo of the key features. The full deployment happens on the 22nd, but your board sees the work and can give feedback. You're not promising them a finished product. You're promising them visibility and a clear path."

> **Sebastian:** "Hmm. That's... not bad. What would the preview include?"

> **Maria:** "The dashboard, the data migration preview, and the user flow. The things that matter most to a board."

> **Sebastian:** "And the bugs?"

> **Maria:** "The bugs are in the admin area. Your board won't see them. Your team will, before the 22nd, and we'll have them fixed."

> **Sebastian:** "OK. Send me a proposal by end of day tomorrow. Make it tight — I need to brief my CEO on Thursday."

> **Maria:** "I'll have it in your inbox by 5 PM tomorrow."

## Scene 4: After the call (4 min)

> **Tom:** "That was good. You didn't take the bait. You acknowledged, you explained, you proposed a solution that didn't exist before."

> **Maria:** "Thanks. I was nervous. He's intense."

> **Tom:** "He's German. Directness is cultural, not personal. The preview idea was clever. You're giving him something for the board, but you're protecting the team from a rushed release."

> **Maria:** "I'm worried he'll come back and ask for more. 'Oh, since you're showing them the preview, can you also add X?'"

> **Tom:** "He might. If he does, what's your answer?"

> **Maria:** "I think... I push back? Politely. 'That's a separate scope. We can quote it for the next phase.' But I'd want to check with you first."

> **Tom:** "Right answer. The rule: never agree to scope changes in the moment. Always say 'let me check with the team and come back with a quote.' That gives you time to think and to protect the team from burnout."

> **Maria:** "Note to self."

> **Tom:** "One more thing. You said 'I want to be honest with you' — that was good. Honesty in UK business is rare. People value it. It builds trust. Keep doing that."

### Vocabulario emergente

| English | Pronunciation | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| to push back | /pʊʃ bæk/ | discrepar, cuestionar |
| scope | /skəʊp/ | alcance, ámbito |
- **"Scope changes"** = cambios de alcance
- **"Quote"** = cotización, presupuesto
- **"The next phase"** = la siguiente fase
- **"Come back with a quote"** = volver con un presupuesto

## Gramática: gerundios vs infinitivos

> **Maria:** "I'm worried he'll come back and ask for more."

Pattern: `to be worried + (that) + clause` para expresar preocupación.

> **Tom:** "Never agree to scope changes in the moment."

Pattern: `agree + to + infinitive` para verbos de intención (agree, decide, refuse, offer, promise).

- "I agreed to take the project."
- "She refused to sign."
- "He offered to help."

> **Maria:** "I'll have it in your inbox by 5 PM."

Pattern: `will + have + past participle` = futuro perfecto. "Lo tendrás..."

- "I'll have sent it by tomorrow."
- "She'll have finished by 5."
