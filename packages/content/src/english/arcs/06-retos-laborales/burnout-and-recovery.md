# Episode 6.4: When the work eats you

**Duration:** ~15 minutes
**Target level:** B2-C1
**Setting:** Maria has been pushing hard for months. She has a conversation with Sam about burnout.

---

## Characters

- **Maria** — At the pub, two months after declining the promotion. Tired.
- **Sam** — Concerned friend.

---

## Scene 1: Maria's state (3 min)

> **Sam:** "You look knackered."

> **Maria:** "I am knackered. I'm not sleeping. I'm working weekends. I keep telling myself it's just a busy period, but it's been three months."

> **Sam:** "Maria. That's not a busy period. That's a pattern. How are you feeling, physically?"

> **Maria:** "I wake up tired. I've got this constant headache. My back hurts. I had to cancel a weekend with my friend because I couldn't face seeing anyone. Yesterday I cried in the office bathroom. I'm not proud of that."

> **Sam:** "Don't be ashamed. That's your body telling you to stop."

### Cultura: el burnout en UK

> **Narrator:** "Burnout is officially recognized by the World Health Organization as a syndrome, not just stress. Symptoms: exhaustion, cynicism, reduced performance. In the UK, talking about burnout has become more accepted in recent years. The phrase 'I'm burnt out' is common in workplaces. Companies now offer mental health days, counselling, and quiet quitting options. This doesn't mean the problem is solved — it means the language exists."

## Scene 2: What burnout feels like (4 min)

> **Sam:** "Let me ask you some questions. Answer honestly."

> **Maria:** "Go on."

> **Sam:** "Do you dread going to work most mornings?"

> **Maria:** "Yes."

> **Sam:** "Have you stopped enjoying things you used to enjoy?"

> **Maria:** "Yeah. I used to love cooking. Now I come home, order Deliveroo, eat in front of my laptop."

> **Sam:** "Are you more cynical about your colleagues than you used to be?"

> **Maria:** "Honestly? Yes. I find myself thinking 'why is this person asking me this stupid question' five times a day."

> **Sam:** "Are you getting sick more often?"

> **Maria:** "I've had three colds in two months. Never used to."

> **Sam:** "Right. That sounds like classic burnout. And it's not your fault. It's the job. It's the system. We weren't built to work 60-hour weeks for months on end."

## Scene 3: What to do about it (5 min)

> **Sam:** "Right. Here's what I'd say. This is not advice. This is what I do, and what I wish I'd done earlier."

> **Maria:** "I'm listening."

> **Sam:** "First: see your GP. Get a check-up. Make sure there's nothing physical. Don't be brave about it."

> **Maria:** "Right."

> **Sam:** "Second: talk to your manager. Not in a 'I might quit' way. In a 'I need to talk about workload' way. Frame it as: 'I want to do my best work, and right now I'm not able to. Help me figure out what to drop.'"

> **Maria:** "Hmm. What do I drop?"

> **Sam:** "That's the conversation to have. Tom is a good manager. He'll listen. If he doesn't, that's information."

> **Maria:** "What if he thinks I'm weak?"

> **Sam:** "He won't. He just respected your decision on the promotion. He knows you take work seriously. If anything, asking for help is a sign of strength, not weakness."

> **Maria:** "Right. What else?"

> **Sam:** "Set a hard stop. Pick a time in the evening — say, 6:30 PM — and stop. No laptop. No email. Nothing. The work will still be there tomorrow. The boundary is for you, not for the work."

## Scene 4: The longer view (3 min)

> **Sam:** "Final thing. The job will always ask for more. There's always a deadline, a crisis, a thing that needs to be done yesterday. If you let work set the pace, you'll burn out. You have to set the pace. The work adapts. You adapt slower."

> **Maria:** "I feel like I'm failing if I'm not at 100%."

> **Sam:** "That's the trap. The idea that you have to be at 100% all the time. No one is. The people who look like they are? They're hiding it. Or they're about to crack."

> **Maria:** "How do you stay balanced?"

> **Sam:** "Badly. But I try. I have a rule: no work emails after 7 PM. Sundays are sacred. I see friends. I exercise. None of this is original. But it's the basics that keep me going."

> **Maria:** "I forgot the basics."

> **Sam:** "Then remember them. That's the only job that matters right now."
