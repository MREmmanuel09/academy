# Episode 6.1: The promotion offer

**Duration:** ~15 minutes
**Target level:** B2
**Setting:** Tom offers Maria a senior role. The conversation is in Tom's office on a Tuesday afternoon.

---

## Characters

- **Maria** — Surprised, excited, and anxious.
- **Tom** — Her manager, offering the role honestly.

---

## Scene 1: The setup (3 min)

> **Tom:** "Maria, do you have a sec?"

> **Maria:** "Of course. What's up?"

> **Tom:** "Shut the door. Have a seat."

> **Maria:** "Okay, this is either very good or very bad."

> **Tom:** "[laughs] It's good. We've been talking about restructuring the team, and we want to put you forward as Senior Project Manager. It's a step up from where you are now. More responsibility, more pay, more visibility. Also, more hours."

> **Maria:** "Wow. I don't know what to say."

> **Tom:** "Don't say anything yet. Take a week. Think about it. Read the job description. Talk to your family. We can answer questions anytime. The offer's real, but it's not going anywhere."

## Scene 2: Maria's questions (5 min)

> **Maria:** "Can I ask some questions now? Even though I haven't decided?"

> **Tom:** "Of course."

> **Maria:** "What's the salary range?"

> **Tom:** "It's £65,000 to £72,000 depending on experience. I'd expect you to start at £68."

> **Maria:** "Right. And the hours?"

> **Tom:** "Officially 40. Realistically, 45-50 some weeks. We'd want you in office at least 3 days a week. The rest you can do from home."

> **Maria:** "What does 'more visibility' mean?"

> **Tom:** "You'll be presenting to the leadership team monthly. You might travel to the Berlin office once a quarter. You'd represent the team at industry events."

> **Maria:** "Will I still have a team, or is this more solo?"

> **Tom:** "You'll have a small team — two mid-level PMs. But the work itself is more strategic, less hands-on. You'll be setting direction rather than running every project yourself."

> **Maria:** "What's the hardest part of the role?"

> **Tom:** "Honestly? The politics. Senior PMs have to navigate a lot of competing interests. Different stakeholders wanting different things. You'll need to make calls that not everyone will like."

## Scene 3: Maria's hesitation (3 min)

> **Maria:** "Can I be honest with you, Tom?"

> **Tom:** "Always."

> **Maria:** "I'm worried I'll burn out. I've been here a year. I love the work. But the last three months have been intense. If I add more, I'm not sure I can sustain it."

> **Tom:** "That's a fair concern. Have you talked to anyone about the intensity?"

> **Maria:** "Not really. I just kept going."

> **Tom:** "Right. Here's what I'd say: the role is more responsibility, but it's also less reactive. Right now, you're putting out fires every week. The senior role is more about strategy, less about daily crisis. The hours may go up a bit, but the nature of the work changes."

> **Maria:** "That's... actually a useful reframe."

> **Tom:** "But if you say yes and you're drowning in three months, tell me. We can adjust. I'd rather you succeed than burn out."

## Scene 4: Maria asks for time (4 min)

> **Maria:** "Can I take a couple of weeks, actually? Not just one. There's stuff I need to think about with my partner, my family."

> **Tom:** "Of course. Take two weeks. We can stretch to three if you need it. The formal offer will be in writing by Friday. The decision deadline can be flexible."

> **Maria:** "Thank you. I really appreciate you being open about the downsides."

> **Tom:** "I'm not going to sell you a fantasy. The role is great. It's also hard. You should know both before you decide."

> **Maria:** "Last question. Why me?"

> **Tom:** "Honestly? You ask the right questions. You push back when something doesn't make sense. You care about the team. And you do the work. That combination is rare."

> **Maria:** "That's... really kind of you to say."

> **Tom:** "It's not kindness. It's observation. Take your two weeks. We'll talk."

## Frases clave para ofertas de trabajo

**"Take a week / Take your time"** = tómate tu tiempo. Estándar.

**"Read the job description"** = lee la descripción del puesto. Antes de aceptar, entérate de qué se trata.

**"What's the salary range?"** = cuál es el rango salarial. Aceptable preguntar, pero espera a que te hagan la oferta formal.

**"What does the role really look like day-to-day?"** = cómo es el día a día. Pregunta poderosa.

**"What's the hardest part of the role?"** = cuál es la parte más difícil. Demuestra que no eres ingenuo.

**"What does success look like in 6 months?"** = cómo se ve el éxito en 6 meses. Muestra que piensas a largo plazo.

**"I'd like to take two weeks to discuss with my family"** = me gustaría tomarme dos semanas para hablarlo con mi familia. Estándar y bien visto.
