# Episode 6.5: The ethical line

**Duration:** ~15 minutes
**Target level:** C1
**Setting:** A year and a half into the job, Maria discovers something ethically wrong. The hardest conversation she's had.

---

## Characters

- **Maria** — Discovered the issue. Stressed but determined.
- **Tom** — Her manager. Will be tested.
- **Whistleblower hotline (anonymous)** — Standard UK reporting channel.

---

## Scene 1: The discovery (3 min)

> **Maria (thinking, alone):** "I found the discrepancy last Tuesday. The financial report shows X. The actual data shows Y. The difference is £180,000. That's not a rounding error. Someone is moving money to a place it shouldn't be. I tried to find an explanation. There isn't one."

> **Maria (to Sam, by text):** "Can we meet? It's not personal. But I need to talk to someone who isn't at work."

### Cultura: whistleblowing en UK

> **Narrator:** "Whistleblowing is when an employee reports wrongdoing at work. The UK has a strong legal framework to protect whistleblowers. The Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998 protects workers from retaliation. Most large companies have anonymous hotlines. The first step is usually to report internally (to a manager or compliance). If that fails or isn't appropriate, you can go to external regulators (FCA, ICO, etc.) or the press. Maria is doing the right thing: she's talking to someone outside work first, before deciding what to do."

## Scene 2: Maria talks to Sam (3 min)

> **Sam:** "Right, what's going on?"

> **Maria:** "I found something. A financial discrepancy. £180,000."

> **Sam:** "Right. How sure are you?"

> **Maria:** "100%. I checked three times. Asked two people to look. We all see the same thing."

> **Sam:** "OK. Have you told anyone at work?"

> **Maria:** "No. I wanted to talk to you first. Get my head straight."

> **Sam:** "Good. Right. Two questions. Is the money moving now, or is it a past error?"

> **Maria:** "It looks ongoing. The pattern started six months ago."

> **Sam:** "OK. Second question. Who's involved?"

> **Maria:** "I don't know. It could be my colleagues. It could be someone above me. Could be Tom. Could be the CFO. I have no idea."

> **Sam:** "Right. That changes the conversation. If it's Tom, this gets complicated."

## Scene 3: What to do (5 min)

> **Sam:** "OK. Step by step. There's a right way and a wrong way to do this."

> **Maria:** "Tell me."

> **Sam:** "Wrong way: go straight to Tom. If he's involved, he covers it up or worse. If he's not involved, he has to investigate, and now you're a target because you're the one who raised it."

> **Maria:** "Right."

> **Sam:** "Right way: use the anonymous hotline. Most companies have one. You can report without naming yourself. They have to investigate. If they don't, you can escalate to external regulators."

> **Maria:** "What if they trace it back to me?"

> **Sam:** "UK law protects whistleblowers. Retaliation is illegal. They could fire you, but you'd have a strong case at an employment tribunal. In practice, the bigger risk is that nothing happens. Companies often cover up quietly. But you have to try."

> **Maria:** "What if I'm wrong? What if there's an explanation I'm missing?"

> **Sam:** "Then the investigation finds it. You're not accusing anyone. You're raising a concern. That's different."

### Cultura: el "raise a concern" vs "whistleblow"

> **Narrator:** "There's a difference between raising a concern and whistleblowing. Raising a concern is the proper, internal, often anonymous step. Whistleblowing is when you go external — to regulators, the press, or the public. Most cases get resolved at the concern stage. Whistleblowing is the last resort. Both are protected in the UK."

## Scene 4: Maria decides (4 min)

> **Maria:** "OK. I'm going to use the hotline tonight. Anonymous. I just describe what I found."

> **Sam:** "Good. Keep a copy of everything. The data you have. The dates. The conversations. If this goes anywhere, you'll need a record."

> **Maria:** "How long does this take to resolve?"

> **Sam:** "Hard to say. Could be weeks. Could be months. Internal investigations are slow. Meanwhile, you keep your head down. Do your job. Don't talk to anyone at work about this."

> **Maria:** "What if they figure out it was me?"

> **Sam:** "Then you tell them: 'I raised a legitimate concern through the proper channels. I did my job.' That's all you need to say."

> **Maria:** "I'm scared."

> **Sam:** "Of course you are. That's the right feeling. If you weren't scared, you wouldn't be paying attention. But you're doing the right thing. That's what matters."

### Cultura: el peso emocional

> **Narrator:** "Whistleblowing is lonely. The person who raises a concern often feels isolated, scared, and unsure. The legal framework exists, but the human cost is real. Sam is doing something important here: he's not telling Maria it's easy. He's telling her it's right. That's the difference between advice and support."
