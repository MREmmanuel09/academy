# Episode 6.3: Saying no without burning bridges

**Duration:** ~13 minutes
**Target level:** B2-C1
**Setting:** Maria decides not to take the senior role. She tells Tom.

---

## Characters

- **Maria** — Has decided.
- **Tom** — Her manager. Disappointed but understanding.

---

## Scene 1: The decision (3 min)

> **Maria:** "Tom, do you have a few minutes?"

> **Tom:** "Always. Sit."

> **Maria:** "I've been thinking about the senior role. A lot. I talked to my partner, my family. I want to be honest with you — I'm going to decline."

> **Tom:** "Okay. I appreciate you taking the time. Can you tell me why?"

> **Maria:** "Several reasons. The biggest one: I don't want to be a bad senior PM by being a good mid-level one. Right now, I'm hands-on, I know my projects in detail, my team trusts me. If I take this role, I'll be in meetings half the time. The team loses someone who's in the trenches with them."

### Vocabulario emergente

| English | Pronunciation | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| to decline | /dɪˈklaɪn/ | rechazar, declinar |
- **"I've been thinking about"** = he estado pensando en
- **"I want to be honest"** = quiero ser honesta
- **"By being"** = por (al) ser
- **"Hands-on"** = práctico, directo
- **"In the trenches"** = en las trincheras, en el día a día

### Cultura: rechazar una oferta en UK

> **Narrator:** "Saying no to a promotion is delicate. In the UK, the safe approach is:
> 1. Be specific about your reasons (not 'I just don't want it')
> 2. Show you thought about it carefully
> 3. Show what you DO want
> 4. Commit to your current role continuing to be great
> 5. Leave the door open for the future
>
> Tom is a good manager. He will be disappointed but he will respect the decision. A bad manager would guilt-trip Maria or hold it against her. We'll see which Tom is."

---

## Scene 2: Tom responds (4 min)

> **Tom:** "I hear you. Honestly, I thought you might say this."

> **Maria:** "You did?"

> **Tom:** "Yeah. You're a good PM, but you've also been a bit stretched the last few months. I was watching to see if you'd flag it. You didn't, until I asked."

> **Maria:** "Right. I should have said something earlier."

> **Tom:** "Probably. But you're saying it now. That's what matters."

> **Maria:** "What does this mean for the role? For the team?"

> **Tom:** "We'll look externally, or internally. It doesn't have to be you. Don't worry about that. The team stays the same. Your projects stay the same. The only thing that changes is I keep looking for a senior PM, and you keep doing your excellent work as mid-level."

> **Maria:** "I really appreciate you being so chill about it."

> **Tom:** "I want you here, doing what you do well, not burned out and resentful. That's a worse outcome for everyone."

| English | Pronunciation | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| stretched | /stretʃt/ | estirado, sobrecargado |
| to flag something | /flæɡ ˈsʌmθɪŋ/ | marcar, señalar algo |

- **"Look externally / internally"** = buscar fuera / dentro de la empresa
- **"Keep doing your excellent work"** = sigue haciendo tu excelente trabajo

- **"Being so chill about it"** = estar tan tranquilo al respecto

### Cultura: las respuestas del manager

> **Narrator:** "Tom is being a good manager. He's not guilting Maria. He's not saying 'you're making a mistake'. He's not saying 'you'll regret this'. He's saying 'I respect your decision, the team stays the same, your work continues to be valued.' This is the mature response. In bad workplaces, declining a promotion can damage your career. In good ones, it builds trust — it shows you know yourself, which is a leadership quality in itself."

---

## Scene 3: Maria sets up for the future (3 min)

> **Maria:** "Can I ask you one more thing?"

> **Tom:** "Go on."

> **Maria:** "Would you mentor me? Not for a promotion, just for my own development. I want to keep growing without changing roles."

> **Tom:** "Absolutely. Let's set up a monthly one-to-one outside the regular ones. Just for your growth. We'll talk about what you want to learn, who you want to learn from, and how to get there."

> **Maria:** "That's perfect. Thank you."

> **Tom:** "Don't thank me yet. Mentorship is a real commitment from me. I'll make time for you if you make time for yourself."

- **"Just for your growth"** = solo para tu crecimiento

## Scene 4: The follow-up (3 min)

> **Tom:** "One last thing. I'm going to need to tell HR that you've declined. Is there anything you want me to communicate on your behalf? Anything I should mention?"

> **Maria:** "Just that I want to keep contributing at the level I'm at. And that I appreciate the trust."

> **Tom:** "Got it. I'll keep it simple. 'Maria considered it carefully. She's staying in her current role. We're supporting her development.' Anything else?"

> **Maria:** "No, that's perfect. Thanks, Tom."

> **Tom:** "Now go enjoy your weekend. And don't work late tonight."

> **Maria:** "Deal."

### Frase clave

**"On your behalf"** = en tu nombre. Para cuando quieres que alguien comunique algo por ti.

- "I'll call him on your behalf."
- "She accepted the award on his behalf."

## Gramática: cleft sentences para énfasis

> **Tom:** "What it comes down to is this: I want you here, doing what you do well."

Pattern: `What + clause + is + noun phrase` = "lo que pasa es que..."

- "What matters is the relationship." (lo que importa es la relación)
- "What I mean is, this is a good team." (lo que quiero decir es que este es un buen equipo)
- "What I'd want is a clear timeline." (lo que querría es un cronograma claro)

> **Tom:** "It's not that I don't think you can do it. It's that I want you to make the right call for you."

Pattern: `It's not that X. It's that Y.` = "No es que X. Es que Y."

- "It's not that I don't like the project. It's that the timing is wrong."
- "It's not that I'm not interested. It's that I can't commit right now."
