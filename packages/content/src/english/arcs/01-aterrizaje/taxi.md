# Episode 1.2: Finding the taxi

**Duration:** ~10 minutes
**Target level:** A0-A1
**Setting:** Outside Heathrow Airport, taxi rank. Maria needs to get to her hotel.

---

## Characters in this episode

- **Maria** — Tired from the flight, but happy to be in London.
- **Taxi Driver** — A friendly Londoner in his 50s. Speaks clearly, asks a few questions.

## Scene 2: In the taxi (4 min)

**[Audio: car engine starting, gentle traffic sounds]**

> **Maria:** "Hi! I'm going to The Strand Hotel, please. It's in central London, near Trafalgar Square."

> **Taxi Driver:** "The Strand Hotel, sure. First time in London?"

> **Maria:** "Yes, it is. I'm starting a new job here."

> **Taxi Driver:** "Welcome! Where are you from?"

> **Maria:** "I'm from Madrid, in Spain."

> **Taxi Driver:** "Ah, beautiful city! How long is the flight?"

> **Maria:** "About two and a half hours."

> **Taxi Driver:** "Not bad. We'll be at the hotel in about forty-five minutes, depending on traffic."

### Grammar note: prepositions of place

> "**In** Madrid" (city)
> "**In** Spain" (country)
> "**Near** Trafalgar Square" (next to)
> "**At** the hotel" (specific location)

Common prepositions of place:
- **in** = dentro de, en (large areas: city, country, room)
- **at** = en (specific point: address, building)
- **near** = cerca de
- **next to** = al lado de
- **between** = entre (two things)
- **in front of** = enfrente de
- **behind** = detrás de
- **on** = sobre, encima de (surface)
- **under** = debajo de

## Scene 3: Small talk (3 min)

**[Audio: gentle traffic, occasional indicator clicks]**

> **Taxi Driver:** "So, what do you do? What's the job?"

> **Maria:** "I'm a project manager. I work for a tech company."

### Grammar note: present simple for habits and facts

> "I **work** for a tech company." (regular job, fact)
> "I **am** a project manager." (current state)
> "We **build** an app." (current activity, fact)
> "I **speak** Spanish." (ability)

We use present simple for:
- Habits: "I drink coffee every morning."
- Facts: "Water boils at 100 degrees."
- Permanent states: "I live in London."
- Scheduled events: "The train leaves at 8."

## Cultural note: tipping in the UK

> Tipping in London taxis is customary but not as high as in the US. Most people round up to the nearest pound or add 10%. Maria didn't tip in this scene — but in real life, rounding £35 to £40 would be friendly.

> **"Cheers"** has many meanings in British English: thanks, goodbye, or "yes please" (when offered a drink). Don't confuse it with the American "cheers!" for celebration.
