### Grammar note: imperatives for directions

> "**Walk** straight ahead."
> "**Turn** left at the traffic lights."

We use imperatives (base form) to give directions. Common ones:

| English | Spanish |
|---|---|
| Go straight | Sigue recto |
| Turn left / right | Gira a la izquierda / derecha |
| Walk two blocks | Camina dos cuadras |
| Take the first left | Toma la primera a la izquierda |
| Cross the street | Cruza la calle |
| Stop at the lights | Para en el semáforo |
| It's on your right | Está a tu derecha |
| Go past the bank | Pasa el banco |
| You'll see it on the corner | Lo verás en la esquina |

### Grammar note: present continuous for current actions

> "Some are **taking** photos, some are **sitting** by the fountains, and some are **walking** around."

We use present continuous (am/is/are + gerund) for actions happening right now:

- "She **is reading** a book." (now)
- "They **are walking** to the office." (now)
- "I **am looking** for the bank." (now)

Common time markers: now, right now, at the moment, currently, today.

## Cultural note: the politeness of "Excuse me" vs "Sorry"

> British English uses both, but with different functions:
> - "**Excuse me**" = attracting attention politely (Excuse me, could you help me?)
> - "**Sorry**" = apologizing for a small inconvenience (Sorry, I bumped into you)

> Americans often use "sorry" for "excuse me". Both work, but in the UK, the distinction is stronger.
