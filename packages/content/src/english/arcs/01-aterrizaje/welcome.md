# Episode 1.1: Welcome to Heathrow

**Duration:** ~12 minutes
**Target level:** A0-A1
**Setting:** Heathrow Airport, London. The plane has just landed.

---

### Grammar note: present simple with "to be"

> "Maria **is** a businesswoman. She **is** from Madrid."

We use **"is"** with: he, she, it, singular nouns.
We use **"are"** with: you, we, they, plural nouns.
We use **"am"** with: I.

### Grammar note: wh-questions

The officer asks three questions:
- "**What**'s the purpose of your visit?" → asks about a thing
- "**How long** are you staying?" → asks about duration

In English, wh-questions start with a question word. Common ones:
- **What** = qué
- **Where** = dónde
- **When** = cuándo
- **Why** = por qué
- **How** = cómo
- **How long** = cuánto tiempo
- **How much** = cuánto (no contable)
- **How many** = cuántos (contable)

### Grammar note: imperatives

> "**Let me** look at the screen."

Imperatives are commands or suggestions:
- Affirmative: "Look!", "Wait!", "Have a seat!"
- Negative: "Don't worry!", "Don't run!"
- Polite suggestion: "Let me check", "Let's go"

We use the base form of the verb (no subject, no -s).

### Grammar note: short answers

> "Do you have anything to declare?"
> "**No, I don't.**"

In English, short answers repeat the auxiliary verb:
- "Do you speak English?" → "Yes, I do." / "No, I don't."
- "Are you tired?" → "Yes, I am." / "No, I'm not."
- "Have you been to London?" → "Yes, I have." / "No, I haven't."

Just saying "Yes" or "No" alone is considered impolite in English.

## Cultural note

> In the UK, even in casual situations, people say "please" and "thank you" constantly. Saying "tea, please" instead of just "tea" is normal. Not saying "please" can be considered rude. This might feel excessive at first, but it will become natural with practice.
