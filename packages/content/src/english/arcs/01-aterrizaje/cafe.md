### Grammar note: "Can I have...?" for ordering

> "**Can I have** a flat white, please?"
> "**I'll have** a croissant."

Two common ways to order food/drink:

| Polite | Casual | Direct |
|---|---|---|
| "Can I have a coffee, please?" | "I'll have a coffee." | "Coffee, please." |
| "Could I get a coffee?" | "Give me a coffee." | "One coffee." |

In real British English, "Can I have...?" is the most common polite form.

### Grammar note: present perfect for life experiences

> "I **went** to Barcelona last year." (specific past time)

Use **past simple** for specific moments in the past:
- "I **went** to Barcelona last year."
- "I **arrived** today."
- "I **loved** the food."

Use **present perfect** for life experiences up to now (no specific time):
- "I **have been** to Spain." (ever in my life)
- "I **have never tried** Chinese food."

Common time markers:
- Past simple: yesterday, last week, in 2020, when I was a child
- Present perfect: ever, never, already, yet, recently, so far

## Cultural note: "Can I have...?" vs "I'll have..."

> In a British café, both work. "Can I have...?" is slightly more polite. The barista uses both naturally with different customers.

> "For here or to go?" is a phrase that signals the barista is asking whether you want to eat in the café or take the food away. In British English, you can also say "to stay" or "to take away".
