# Episode 3.3: The meeting where nobody said anything

**Duration:** ~16 minutes
**Target level:** B1
**Setting:** Conference room, 11:00 AM. A project status meeting with the GymTotal client.

---

## Characters in this episode

- **Maria** — Facilitating her first meeting.
- **Tom** — Supporting her, but letting her lead.
- **Daniel** (the client) — Spanish, but speaks English well. Skeptical.
- **Sophie** (client's colleague) — Quiet, taking notes.

---

## Scene 1: The silence (5 min)

**[Audio: meeting room, projector humming]**

> **Maria:** "Right, thank you all for joining. So, to recap, the timeline is: design by next Friday, copy by the following Monday, dev starts the 15th. Daniel, does that work for you?"

> **Daniel:** "Hmm."

> **Maria:** "…"

> **Daniel:** "It could work. Let me think."

> **Tom:** "Take your time."

**[3 seconds of silence]**

> **Maria:** "Okay. While Daniel thinks, let me show you the design concepts. Sophie, you'll be reviewing these — let me know if you have questions…"

### Narrator

> **Narrator:** "What just happened? Daniel said 'hmm' and then silence. In Spanish, this is a bit awkward. In English meetings, this is normal. People need time to think. Maria almost filled the silence — but that's a mistake. In UK culture, rushing to fill silence makes you seem nervous. Better to let the silence sit."

> **Tom** (later, privately): "You did well. The silence thing — Spanish speakers really struggle with that. But you handled it. One tip: when someone says 'I'll think about it,' write it down as an action item. Otherwise it disappears."

## Scene 2: Disagreeing without conflict (6 min)

> **Daniel:** "I've been thinking about the design. I'm not sure the colour scheme is right for our brand."

> **Maria:** "Okay. Can you tell me more?"

> **Daniel:** "It's too corporate. We want something more energetic. Something that makes people want to move."

> **Maria:** "I see what you mean. Let me push back a little, though — we tested similar palettes with your target audience last month, and they responded well to the corporate feel. Trust and reliability matter a lot in fitness, especially for older demographics."

> **Daniel:** "But our gym is for young people. 20 to 35."

> **Maria:** "Fair point. What if we keep the corporate base but add a more energetic accent colour? That way we get the trust signal for the older end, and the energy for the younger end. Does that work?"

> **Daniel:** "Hmm. Let me see a mockup of that."

> **Maria:** "Sure. I'll have Priya put something together by Thursday."

### Frase clave: cómo discrepar sin conflicto

> **Narrator:** "Notice how Maria disagrees with Daniel. She doesn't say 'You're wrong' or 'I disagree.' She uses softeners:

| Spanish-style (direct) | English-style (soft) |
|---|---|
| "No estoy de acuerdo." | "Let me push back a little…" |
| "Eso es un error." | "I see what you mean, but…" |
| "Eso no funciona." | "That could be tricky because…" |
| "¿Por qué no…?" | "What if we…?" / "Have you considered…?" |
| "No, eso no." | "I'm not sure that would work for us because…" |

> **Tom** (later): "That was a masterclass in disagreement. The key is: 'I see your point, but here's a third option.' You're not saying no, you're redirecting. The client feels heard, you protect the project."

## Scene 3: The "British no" (5 min)

> **Sophie:** "One more thing. Can we add a live chat to the website? It's very important for our conversion rate."

> **Maria:** "Sophie, that's a great idea. Let me check with the dev team — I'm not sure we have the budget for that in this phase."

> **Sophie:** "Okay."

> **Daniel:** "If you can't do it, just say so. We can move it to phase two."

> **Maria:** "I'll get back to you on Friday. But yes, it's possible we move it."

> **Tom** (after the meeting): "Sophie asked for something. You said 'I'll check.' She heard 'no.' That's a 'British no.' Saying 'I'll check' or 'it's possible' usually means 'no, but politely.' If you wanted to say yes, you'd say 'yes, we can do that.' When in doubt, British people will assume the worst."

> **Maria:** "So 'I'll get back to you' = 'no'?"

> **Tom:** "Often. Unless followed by an actual timeline. 'I'll get back to you by Friday' is a real maybe. 'I'll get back to you' with no date is a soft no."

### The "British no" cheat sheet

| What they say | What they mean |
|---|---|
| "I'll have to check." | No. |
| "That's an interesting idea." | No. |
| "I'm not sure that's something we can do." | No. |
| "Let me get back to you." (sin fecha) | Probablemente no. |
| "We'll see." | No. |
| "It's not ideal." | Es bastante malo. |
| "With respect…" | Te equivocas. |
| "It's not bad." | No me gusta pero no quiero decir "no" directamente. |

> **Tom:** "We do this because we'd rather seem vague than rude. But the cost is confusion. When you say 'I'll check,' be ready that people hear 'no.' Be specific: 'I'll check and get back to you by Friday.'"

## Self-assessment

- [ ] Sé dar mi opinión en una reunión sin sonar conflictivo.
- [ ] Reconozco cuándo alguien está diciendo "no" indirectamente en inglés.
- [ ] Sé usar al menos 3 softeners: "I see what you mean, but…", "Let me push back…", "What if…?"
- [ ] No intento llenar los silencios en una reunión.
- [ ] Conozco la diferencia entre "I'll check" y "I'll check by Friday".

## Comprehension quiz (3 min)

1. **Why shouldn't Maria fill the silence after "Hmm"?**
   - [ ] Silence is rude in the UK
   - [ ] Rushing to fill it seems nervous; people need time to think
   - [ ] The client left
   - [ ] Tom told her to stay quiet always

2. **How did Maria disagree well with Daniel?**
   - [ ] Saying "You're wrong"
   - [ ] Softeners + a third option ("I see what you mean, but…")
   - [ ] Staying silent
   - [ ] Changing the subject

3. **"I'll check" with no date usually means…**
   - [ ] Yes
   - [ ] A soft no
   - [ ] Maybe next year
   - [ ] Ask again louder

4. **When Daniel said "I'll think about it", Maria should…**
   - [ ] Forget it
   - [ ] Write it down as an action item
   - [ ] Decide for him
   - [ ] End the meeting

5. **"What if we keep the base but add an accent colour?" is…**
   - [ ] A direct no
   - [ ] Redirecting: acknowledging + proposing a third way
   - [ ] Changing topic
   - [ ] Flattery

### Answers

1. Rushing to fill it seems nervous; people need time to think · 2. Softeners + a third option ("I see what you mean, but…") · 3. A soft no · 4. Write it down as an action item · 5. Redirecting: acknowledging + proposing a third way
