# Episode 3.2: The email you wish you hadn't sent

**Duration:** ~14 minutes
**Target level:** A2-B1
**Setting:** Maria's desk, end of her first day. 17:30.

---

## Characters in this episode

- **Maria** — Tired, eager to make a good impression.
- **Tom** — Her team lead, who gives feedback on her draft.

---

## Scene 1: The draft (4 min)

**[Audio: keyboard typing, office emptying]**

> **Narrator:** "It's the end of Maria's first day. Tom asked her to send a follow-up email to a potential client — a Spanish gym chain called GymTotal. The client had emailed in Spanish, so Maria wrote back in Spanish. But Tom wants her to translate it into English for the team. She opens her email."

> **Maria's draft (on screen):**

```
Dear Mr. Rodriguez,

Thank you very much for your email and for your interest in our services. We are very interested in working with you.

I am writing to confirm that we received your information and we are reviewing it. We will send you a proposal next week.

If you have any questions, please do not hesitate to contact me.

I look forward to hearing from you.

Best regards,
Maria Garcia
Project Manager
Lumen Marketing
```

> **Narrator:** "Maria is about to send it. But Tom stops by her desk."

> **Tom:** "How's the email going?"

> **Maria:** "Almost done. I was about to send it."

> **Tom:** "Mind if I take a look before you hit send?"

> **Maria:** "Of course. Please."

> **Tom:** "… Okay. This is fine, but it's a bit formal. Also, you don't need to say 'we received your information' — we both know that. Let me show you how I would write it."

## Scene 2: Tom's version (5 min)

```
Hi Daniel,

Thanks for reaching out.

We're reviewing your brief now and will send a proposal by Friday. Let me know if you have any questions in the meantime.

Best,
Maria
```

> **Tom:** "See? Shorter. Friendlier. We don't know him yet, but 'Hi' is fine — it's a first contact, not a legal document."

> **Maria:** "But isn't 'Dear Mr. Rodriguez' more polite?"

> **Tom:** "In Spain, maybe. In the UK, that level of formality can actually feel cold. We tend to use first names pretty quickly in business. Wait until he signs back, then move to 'Hi Daniel'."

> **Maria:** "Got it. And I don't need all the long sentences?"

> **Tom:** "Right. In English, the rule is: if the reader can guess it, don't write it. 'We received your information' — of course you did, you sent it yesterday. Skip that. Get to the point. They'll appreciate it."

> **Maria:** "Okay. Less is more."

> **Tom:** "Exactly. You'll get the hang of it."

### Frase clave

**"Thanks for reaching out."** — Forma estándar de responder a un primer contacto (email, mensaje, formulario). Más natural que "Thank you for your email".

Variaciones:
- "Thanks for getting in touch."
- "Thanks for the message."
- (Si es un formulario de la web:) "Thanks for your enquiry."

## Scene 3: Grammar focus — formal vs. informal register (5 min)

> **Narrator:** "Notice how Tom's version is shorter and uses different words. English has a 'register' — how formal or casual you sound. In business emails, the UK style is usually:

**1. Shorter sentences**
- Formal: *"I am writing to confirm that we received your information."*
- Casual: *"Got it, thanks."*

**2. First names early**
- Formal: *"Dear Mr. Rodriguez"*
- Casual: *"Hi Daniel"*

**3. Modal verbs for politeness**
- "Could you…" / "Would you mind…" (more polite)
- "Can you…" (less polite, but okay with colleagues)
- "Do this" (rude, only with close teammates)

**4. Softeners**
- "I was wondering if…" / "Just a quick question…" / "When you have a moment…"

> **Tom:** "One more thing — watch your subject line. Never leave it empty. 'Following up' is fine. 'Quick question' is fine. 'Hello' alone is a recipe for being ignored."

> **Maria:** "Noted. 'Hello' alone is a crime."

> **Tom:** "Ha. In London, it is."

### Modismos del episodio

- **"Less is more"** — Menos es más. Principio de brevedad y elegancia.
- **"Get the hang of it"** — Aprender a hacer algo con práctica.
- **"Reach out"** — Contactar. Muy común en emails profesionales.
- **"In the meantime"** — Mientras tanto.
