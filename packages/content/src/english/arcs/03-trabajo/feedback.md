# Episode 3.5: Receiving feedback (and not crying)

**Duration:** ~14 minutes
**Target level:** B1-B2
**Setting:** A 1:1 review meeting with Tom, end of Maria's third month.

---

## Characters

- **Maria** — Has been at the company 3 months. Has worked hard.
- **Tom** — Her manager. About to give her a performance review.

---

## Scene 1: The setup (3 min)

> **Tom:** "Maria, before we start, I want to set the tone for this meeting. This isn't a 'you're fired' conversation. This is a 1:1 review. We're going to talk about what's working, what's not, and what we want to do next. Okay?"

> **Maria:** "Okay."

> **Tom:** "And — this is the most important thing — my feedback isn't personal. It's about the work. If I say 'the report was late,' I'm not saying you're a bad person. I'm saying the report was late. We're going to be specific. We're not going to be mean. Deal?"

> **Maria:** "Deal."

> **Tom:** "Cool. So, three months in. Overall, you're doing well. The team likes you, the clients like you, and you've shipped two projects on time. That's a strong start."

## Scene 2: The hard part (6 min)

> **Tom:** "Now the harder stuff. There's one pattern I want to flag. When you get pushback from clients, you sometimes go quiet and then send a follow-up email agreeing to what they said — even if you disagreed in the meeting. That's a problem, because then I get a confused message."

> **Maria:** "I was trying to be polite."

> **Tom:** "I get that. And in Spain, that might be the right call. But here, if you disagree in a meeting, I need to know that. Otherwise, when I forward the email, I'm sending mixed signals to the client. They think we agree. We don't."

> **Maria:** "…"

> **Tom:** "I want to be clear: this isn't a fireable offence. It's a pattern, and patterns can change. I want to work on it with you. Sound okay?"

> **Maria:** "Yes. I'm sorry."

> **Tom:** "Don't apologize. Just take it as a data point. What are you going to do differently next time?"

> **Maria:** "I think… speak up in the meeting, even if I disagree. And if I'm not sure, ask for 24 hours to think instead of agreeing on the spot."

> **Tom:** "Exactly. That's perfect. Let's check in on this in a month."

### Frase clave

**"Don't apologize. Just take it as a data point."** — Filosofía del feedback profesional en UK: el feedback no es un ataque, es información para mejorar.

Variaciones:
- "I appreciate you raising it. What can we do about it?"
- "Tell me more about that."
- "How do you want to handle it going forward?"

## Scene 3: When YOU give feedback (5 min)

> **Maria:** "Can I give you some feedback?"

> **Tom:** "Of course. Go ahead."

> **Maria:** "Last week, in the Acme call, you jumped in when I was explaining the timeline. I had it. I was about to finish. It made me feel like you didn't trust me with the client."

> **Tom:** "… Fair. I didn't realize I was doing that. I'm sorry."

> **Maria:** "Don't apologize — take it as a data point."

**[Both laugh]**

> **Tom:** "Okay, fair. I'll watch for that. Thank you for telling me. That takes guts."

### Frase clave

**"Can I give you some feedback?"** — Forma estándar de iniciar feedback en UK oficinas. Diferencia con español:
- En español: "Una cosa, …" o "A ver, te voy a decir algo"
- En inglés: "Can I give you some feedback?" (más formal pero más suave)

### Estructura de feedback en UK oficinas (SBI)

> **Tom:** "One thing I learned early. The framework I use is SBI:

- **S**ituation: When / where it happened
- **B**ehavior: What the person did
- **I**mpact: How it affected you or the project

So if I were giving you feedback: 'In the Acme call last week (S), you agreed to the client's request even though we hadn't agreed internally (B), and I had to send a confusing follow-up (I).'

SBI. Three sentences. No emotion. No 'always' or 'never.' Just one specific moment."

### Modismos del episodio

- **"Take it as a data point"** — Tómalo como información, no como crítica personal.
- **"Check in"** — Reunión breve para ver cómo va algo.
- **"Cool"** — Vale, de acuerdo.
- **"Deal?"** — ¿Trato? Cierra un acuerdo informal.
- **"Sound okay?" / "Sound good?"** — ¿Te parece bien? Para confirmar.
