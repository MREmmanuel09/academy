# Episode 5.1: Edinburgh by train

**Duration:** ~14 minutes
**Target level:** A2
**Setting:** Maria is taking the train from London King's Cross to Edinburgh for a long weekend.

---

## Characters

- **Maria** — Solo traveler, first long train trip in UK.
- **Tom** — Back in London, by text.
- **Platform staff** — Helpful at King's Cross.
- **Stranger on the train** — A friendly Scotsman in his 50s.

---

## Scene 1: At King's Cross (4 min)

**[Audio: train station, announcements, footsteps]**

> **Narrator:** "It's 7 AM. Maria is at King's Cross station, dragging a small suitcase, trying to find platform 9."

> **Maria:** "Excuse me, where's the 11:30 to Edinburgh?"

> **Staff:** "Platform 9, love. Up the stairs on your left, follow the signs."

> **Maria:** "Brilliant. Thanks."

> **[Audio: walking, footsteps on stairs]**

> **Narrator:** "London to Edinburgh on the LNER train takes about four and a half hours. Maria booked her ticket online three weeks ago — £45 one way, off-peak. She has a window seat, a coffee from the station, and a book she's been meaning to read."

### Cultura: tren en UK

> **Narrator:** "The UK has an excellent train network, but it's expensive. Booking in advance is essential — same-day tickets can cost 3-4x more. Apps like Trainline, National Rail, and the operator apps (LNER, Avanti, CrossCountry) help you compare. Off-peak tickets are much cheaper. Always book a specific seat — UK trains get full. Maria's £45 ticket is good value; the same ticket bought on the day could be £150+."

### Cultura: charla en el tren

> **Narrator:** "Trains are one of the few places in the UK where strangers actually talk to each other. Long journeys, you're stuck together. The etiquette is: small talk is OK, deep conversation is fine, but if the other person puts on headphones, you stop. Trains are not as silent as US trains but more reserved than Spanish ones. Iain is being friendly but not intrusive."

> **Maria's voiceover:** "It's funny. In Spain, I would have started this conversation in the first 30 seconds. Here, I waited for him."

> **Iain:** "How long are you in Edinburgh for?"

> **Maria:** "Three nights. Friday to Monday."

> **Iain:** "Plenty of time. The Old Town is a must — the castle, the Royal Mile, Victoria Street. You'll feel like you're in a Harry Potter film."

> **Maria:** "I have to go there."

> **Iain:** "You should. Their coffee's terrible, but the scones are good."

### Frase clave

**"It's a must"** = es algo obligatorio / imprescindible. "The castle is a must" = tienes que ir al castillo.

- "That restaurant is a must-try."
- "The museum is a must-see."
- "The cake is a must-eat." (esto último es jocoso)

## Frases clave para viajes

**"How long are you staying?"** — Cuánto tiempo te quedas. Más natural que "How many days?"

**"I'm here for the weekend"** = estoy aquí el fin de semana. Forma común de responder.

**"I'm on holiday"** vs **"I'm on vacation"** — los dos significan "estoy de vacaciones". UK prefiere "holiday", US prefiere "vacation".

**"I'm just visiting"** = solo estoy de visita. Útil cuando alguien pregunta cuánto tiempo.

**"I haven't been yet"** = no he ido aún. Típica respuesta cuando alguien recomienda un lugar.
