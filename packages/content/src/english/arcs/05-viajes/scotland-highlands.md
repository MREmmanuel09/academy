# Episode 5.3: A day in the Scottish Highlands

**Duration:** ~14 minutes
**Target level:** B1
**Setting:** Maria has booked a day tour to the Highlands from Edinburgh. She's on a small bus with 8 other tourists.

---

## Characters

- **Maria** — Solo on a day tour.
- **Hamish** — The tour guide. Scottish, 40s, dramatic and funny.
- **Various tourists** — A couple from Australia, an older woman from Germany.

---

## Scene 1: The departure (3 min)

**[Audio: bus, chattering tourists]**

> **Hamish:** "Right, everyone! Welcome aboard! I'm Hamish, I'll be your driver, guide, entertainer, and emergency contact for the next twelve hours. We're heading up to Glencoe today. If you've got any questions, fire away. If you've got any complaints, keep them to yourself till the end."

> **[Laughter]**

> **Hamish:** "We've got a beautiful day ahead. Sunscreen, optional. Sense of humour, mandatory. Waterproof jacket, definitely. Scottish weather changes more than my ex's mood."

> **[More laughter]**

> **Maria (murmurando a su compañero):** "He said waterproof jacket, definitely. I don't have one."

### Cultura: tour guides

> **Narrator:** "Tour guides in Scotland are a special breed. They have to be funny, knowledgeable, and tolerant of any weather. Hamish's style is irreverent — 'sense of humour, mandatory' — but it's earned. He knows the Highlands in his bones. Tourists love him because he makes the bus feel like a comedy club, but also because he knows the history deeply. The best guides are part entertainer, part historian, part therapist."

## Scene 2: On the road (4 min)

> **Hamish:** "To your left, you'll see Loch Lomond. That's 'loch', not 'lock'. It means lake. We Scots have our own words for everything, mostly because we wanted to confuse the English, and it's worked for a thousand years."

> **Tourist (Australia):** "So how do you say 'thank you' in Scottish?"

> **Hamish:** "We say 'cheers' for most things. 'Thanks' is fine too. 'Ta' if you're really lazy."

> **Tourist:** "And how do you say 'you're welcome'?"

> **Hamish:** "Usually just 'no bother' or 'no probs'. Or we just nod. Very understated, us Scots."

### Cultura: el idioma escocés

> **Narrator:** "Scotland has three main languages: English (everyone speaks it), Scots (a Germanic language closely related to English, with its own vocabulary and grammar — 'aye' for yes, 'wee' for small, 'bairn' for child), and Scottish Gaelic (a Celtic language spoken mainly in the Highlands and Islands, related to Irish). In Edinburgh and tourist areas, you'll hear Scots words and accents but everyone uses standard English. The accent is part of the charm. Don't pretend to understand everything. Just smile and ask for clarification if needed."

## Scene 3: At Glencoe (4 min)

> **Hamish:** "Right, we've arrived. Glencoe. This is where, in 1692, the Campbell clan massacred the MacDonalds. Thirty-eight people in their beds. Babies, old folks, the lot. King William had ordered it because the MacDonalds had been slow to pledge loyalty. Took three days to die of cold, those who ran into the hills. This is one of the most beautiful and most haunting places in Scotland."

> **[Long pause]**

> **Hamish:** "Right! Who wants lunch? The café does a decent soup. The scones are all right. Coffee is proper Scottish, which means it's hot and tastes of nothing."

> **Tourist (Germany):** "Where can I take good photos?"

> **Hamish:** "Anywhere you point your camera, love. The whole glen is the photo. Just don't stand in the road. We have a strict no-flattened-tourist policy."

### Cultura: historia y humor

> **Narrator:** "Hamish did something clever. He dropped a heavy historical moment — the Glencoe Massacre, one of Scotland's most traumatic events — and then immediately shifted to lunch. This is the British/Scottish guide style: respect the history, but don't be crushed by it. The 'no-flattened-tourist policy' joke is dark, but it's also a real safety message. Tourists do get killed standing in the wrong place in the Highlands. The blend of history, dark humour, and practical advice is the Scottish storytelling tradition."

## Scene 4: On the way back (3 min)

> **Hamish:** "Last call for the toilet before we hit the motorway. After that, it's a four-hour drive back. Feel free to nap, but if you snore, I'll turn the radio up."

> **Maria:** "Quick question, Hamish. Why do you say 'aye' instead of 'yes'?"

> **Hamish:** "We just do. The English stole half our language anyway. 'Yes' comes from Old English 'gēse'. Scots is older. We've been saying 'aye' since before the English had a word for it."

> **Maria:** "Cool."

> **Hamish:** "Cool. Listen. You're Spanish. The Scots and the Spanish have something in common."

> **Maria:** "What's that?"

> **Hamish:** "We both have empires we don't talk about, and weather that doesn't behave."

> **[Laughter]**
