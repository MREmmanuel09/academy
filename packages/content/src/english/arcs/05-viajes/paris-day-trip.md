# Episode 5.4: A day in Paris

**Duration:** ~14 minutes
**Target level:** B1
**Setting:** Maria takes the Eurostar from London to Paris for the day. St Pancras to Gare du Nord, 2h15.

---

## Characters

- **Maria** — On a day trip to Paris.
- **Border officer** — At Gare du Nord.
- **Café waiter** — In a small bistro.
- **Tourist information staff** — At the Eiffel Tower.

### Cultura: Eurostar

> **Narrator:** "The Channel Tunnel (Chunnel) opened in 1994. The Eurostar train runs from London St Pancras to Paris Gare du Nord in 2 hours 15 minutes. Before the tunnel, you had to fly (1 hour flight + 2 hours of airports) or take a ferry (6-8 hours). The train is faster door-to-door than flying. Tickets range from £50 to £250+ depending on how far in advance and which class. Maria booked 3 weeks ahead for £89 return. The passport check happens in London, not at the border. UK is no longer in the EU; you'll go through French immigration (passport) and customs."

**[Audio: train, French announcement]**

> **Maria:** "Merci."

> **Attendant:** "You're welcome. Bonne journée."

> **Maria (murmurando):** "OK, that went well. My French is rustier than I thought, but I got there."

### Cultura: hablar francés básico en París

> **Narrator:** "Parisians have a reputation for being rude to non-French speakers. In reality, most are kind if you try. The rule: start in French, then switch to English. 'Bonjour, excusez-moi, parlez-vous anglais?' is the magic phrase. They appreciate the effort. Maria tried French, stumbled, succeeded. That's how it works. Bonus: French people find Spanish charming. Mentioning you're from Spain almost always gets a smile."

### Cultura: el francés de frontera

> **Narrator:** "Border officers in France are professional and quick. They ask the standard three questions: your nationality, your reason for visiting, and how long you'll stay. Maria answered in French. The officer appreciated it. You don't need to be fluent — basic phrases work. Just be polite, direct, and have your documents ready."

## Scene 4: At the Eiffel Tower (5 min)

**[Audio: street, distant Eiffel Tower sounds]**

> **Maria (in line):** "Bonjour. Un billet, s'il vous plaît. Pour le sommet."

> **Ticket staff:** "Soixante-dix-neuf euros."

> **Maria:** "Soixante-dix-neuf?"

> **Ticket staff:** "Yes, seventy-nine. Card OK?"

> **Maria:** "Oui. Voilà."

> **Tourist staff (in line for the lift):** "Excusez-moi, vous parlez anglais? How long is the wait?"

> **Maria:** "[in English] About 30 minutes, I think. We can see the line. Should be OK."

> **Tourist staff:** "Thank you. Oh, you speak French well!"

> **Maria:** "Pas vraiment. J'essaie."

> **Tourist staff:** "It sounded great. Have a good one."

### Cultura: euros

> **Narrator:** "After Brexit, the UK no longer uses the euro. You'll need to exchange currency before crossing, or use a card with no foreign transaction fees. Most places in Paris take cards. The Eiffel Tower: 29 euros by stairs, 79 euros by lift to the top. The lift is worth it for the view. The stairs are good exercise. Maria chose the lift because she only had 6 hours in Paris."
